The phone system that is one file and one web page

BitVoice is a complete phone system for offices, contact centres and hosted providers: extensions, queues, menus, voicemail, recording, trunks and desk phones, set up entirely in the browser. An open-source alternative to FreePBX and Asterisk, with neither underneath.

Replace FreePBX, keep your numbers and settings

Everything in one program

The technology inside BitVoice

Most phone systems are a stack: Asterisk, a GUI on top, modules bought one at a time, a media server, a transcription service. BitVoice is one program written in Go. Each square is an article about one technology: what it is, how BitVoice does it, and what has been tested.

See it as it happens

Live calls, with their quality

Every call in progress, both sides, with the codec, packet loss, jitter and an audio score, and what needs attention in plain words on the dashboard.

  • A call flow diagram of where calls from outside go
  • A call-path simulator: pick a caller, a number and a time and see the exact route, without ringing anything
  • Call history with recordings, quality and search across what was said

Queues

A wallboard for every queue

Callers waiting, the longest wait and each agent's state, live. Agents log in and pause from their phones or the screen; supervisors listen, whisper and barge.

Routing

Where every call goes, drawn

The main number to the opening hours, then the menu, the queues and the people, each ending where it ends. Change a step and the diagram follows.

Plainly

What is proven, and what is not yet

Every change runs the Go tests with the race detector, fuzzing, and browser tests that make real WebRTC calls and check the sound both ways. BitVoice is checked against Asterisk, libpri, ffmpeg, JsSIP and SIP.js. It has not yet been run with every phone, carrier and telephony card it supports; the proof page lists which.

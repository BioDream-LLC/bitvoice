Proof

How BitVoice is tested, and what has not been tested

A phone system is only as good as the calls it does not drop. This page says what every change must pass, what BitVoice is checked against, and what it has not been tried with yet.

On every change

  • The Go tests, with the race detector, on Linux against both SQLite and PostgreSQL, on Windows and on macOS (continuous integration)
  • Fuzzing of the SIP, SDP, STUN and SRTP parsers, and of the ISDN facility decoder
  • Every setting has a screen: a test walks the whole configuration and fails if any setting is not named in the web interface, so nothing quietly becomes file-only
  • Browser tests in Chromium that drive every screen as a person would, and make real WebRTC calls between browser phones and the engine with the sound checked in both directions

Not yet tried for real

These are built and tested against stand-ins, but not yet with the real thing. Reports from anyone who tries them are welcome on GitHub.

  • Real desk phones: the automatic set-up (Yealink, Poly, Grandstream, Snom, Fanvil, Cisco, Mitel and Aastra, Sangoma, Algo, CyberData) is checked against each maker's published formats, not yet on the phones themselves
  • SIP carriers in production, the outbound dialer's answering-machine detection on real lines, and T.38 fax through real carriers
  • Telephony cards and PRI switches, including transfers handed to the phone company on real lines
  • Skinny, MGCP and H.323 equipment; hotel systems (FIAS) against a real property-management system; SNMP managers
  • Let's Encrypt itself (as against its test authority), TURN through real NATs, and Meta's live WhatsApp and Messenger APIs
  • Published capacity figures on stated hardware
  • The public-safety lab track with a live ESInet, a text control centre, a selective router's CAMA trunks, a P25 system, or a CAD: built and tested against stand-ins, waiting on a design partner's lab

Not built

  • SS7 link sets of several links, a signal transfer point, and the SS7 database lookups (number portability, toll-free)
  • MFC-R2's China variant: OpenR2's own one contradicts itself, so there is nothing sound to test against
  • P25 Phase 2 half-rate voice (AMBE+2): patented until 2028

How BitVoice is tested, and what has not been tested

A phone system is only as good as the calls it does not drop. This page says what every change must pass, what BitVoice is checked against, and what it has not been tried with yet.

On every change

  • The Go tests, with the race detector, on Linux against both SQLite and PostgreSQL, on Windows and on macOS (continuous integration)
  • Fuzzing of the SIP, SDP, STUN and SRTP parsers, and of the ISDN facility decoder
  • Every setting has a screen: a test walks the whole configuration and fails if any setting is not named in the web interface, so nothing quietly becomes file-only
  • Browser tests in Chromium that drive every screen as a person would, and make real WebRTC calls between browser phones and the engine with the sound checked in both directions

Not yet tried for real

Built and tested against stand-ins or the makers' published formats, but not yet with the real thing. Reports from anyone who tries them are welcome on GitHub.

  • Real desk phones: the automatic set-up (Yealink, Poly, Grandstream, Snom, Fanvil, Cisco, Mitel and Aastra, Sangoma, Algo, CyberData) is checked against each maker's published formats, not on the phones themselves
  • Calls in from a commercial carrier (calls out to the phone network through SIP.US work), and T.38 fax through a real carrier
  • Telephony cards on real lines: PRI has run through real DAHDI drivers on Linux, between two software spans
  • Skinny and MGCP equipment, a Microsoft Teams tenant, a hotel's property-management system, Meta's live WhatsApp and Messenger APIs, and the iPhone app on a real iPhone
  • The public-safety lab track with a live ESInet, a text control centre, a selective router's CAMA trunks, a P25 system, or a production CAD: built and tested against stand-ins, waiting on a design partner's lab

Not offered

  • P25 Phase 2 half-rate voice (AMBE+2): patented until 2028
  • Ed448 signatures: not in Go's standard library, and BitVoice writes no cryptography of its own
  • An SS7 signal transfer point and SCCP/TCAP: a PBX is an end point; number portability and toll-free lookups are the carrier's
  • MFC-R2's China variant: OpenR2's own one contradicts itself, so there is nothing sound to test against
  • ALI over PAM or E2: through a gateway
